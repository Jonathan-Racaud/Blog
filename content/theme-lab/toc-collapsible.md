## Why a table of contents

Long articles need orientation. A table of contents turns a wall of text into a navigable document and signals the structure before the reader commits to the whole thing.

### The technical-index idea

The recessed-panel metaphor gives the index a natural home: a small sub-panel with a mono label, treated like the label on a cassette deck rather than a web widget.

### What makes a TOC useful

It must be honest about the document structure, cheap to scan, and quiet enough that it never competes with the prose.

## Placement options

Placement changes the reading experience more than the index itself. These three test pages exist so the options can be compared side by side.

### Inline block

The index sits at the top of the article, inside the reading measure. It is the most mobile-first option and needs no layout surgery.

### Sticky side rail

The index rides beside the text on wide screens and stacks above the article on small screens. It is the most technical-document option, but it costs horizontal space.

### Collapsible block

The index collapses to a single labeled row and expands on demand. It keeps the article visually clean while still offering orientation.

## Design rules

The index must speak the same accent language as the rest of the theme: orange marks the current action, teal marks structure and status.

### One more rule

No decoration for its own sake. If a style does not help scanning, it does not belong in the index.
